# Applications of semialgebraic geometry

Semialgebraic methods apply whenever a problem can be expressed using real polynomial equations and inequalities, either directly or after an exact algebraic reformulation.

## Computational geometry

Many geometric primitives are semialgebraic:

- disks, balls, spheres, cones, cylinders, simplexes, and polyhedra;
- intersections and Boolean combinations of implicit polynomial regions;
- distance and nearest-point conditions;
- collision and containment queries;
- offsets and some Minkowski constructions;
- exact projection and coordinate shadows.

CAD and quantifier elimination can resolve degeneracies that are awkward for purely floating-point geometry, while numerical meshes can be generated afterward for display or simulation.

## Robotics and motion planning

Configuration-space obstacles for mechanisms with algebraic kinematics are often semialgebraic. Collision-free configurations satisfy polynomial inequalities, and connectivity/path questions become geometric questions about the free region.

Useful operations include:

- emptiness and feasibility;
- connected components;
- exact point location;
- projection onto selected configuration variables;
- path/roadmap construction;
- parameter stratification when link lengths or clearances vary.

`semialg` provides exact connectivity and cell-chain paths; full algebraic roadmap algorithms are outside the supported API.

## Formal verification and hybrid systems

Safety properties can often be expressed as universally quantified polynomial implications. For example,

\[
\forall x\;(I(x)\Rightarrow p(x)\ge0)
\]

asks whether an invariant region lies inside a safe set. Quantifier elimination can also eliminate hidden states or parameters.

Polynomial hybrid systems and barrier-certificate conditions naturally generate semialgebraic proof obligations, although large dynamical systems usually require problem-specific structure beyond generic CAD.

## Control and stability

Polynomial Lyapunov conditions involve positivity over semialgebraic domains. Typical questions include:

- is \(V(x)>0\) away from an equilibrium?;
- is \(\dot V(x)<0\) on a constrained domain?;
- for which parameters do these inequalities hold?;
- what is the exact boundary of an admissible parameter set?

Exact QE can answer low-dimensional instances and can certify symbolic parameter regimes. For large systems, sums-of-squares techniques may be preferable; these complement rather than replace exact semialgebraic decision methods.

## Global optimization

Polynomial optimization over polynomial constraints is a central semialgebraic problem. Applications occur in engineering design, geometric fitting, economics, statistics, and scientific modeling.

`semialg` can exploit boxes, polytopes, separability, KKT equations, exact algebraic candidates, and CAD/function-range certification. It is particularly useful when the desired answer is symbolic or algebraic rather than a floating-point approximation.

## Parameterized scientific models

Suppose a model contains parameters \(a,b\) and state variables \(x\). Questions such as

\[
\exists x\;F(x,a,b)=0,
\qquad
\forall x\in S(a,b)\;g(x,a,b)\ge0
\]

define semialgebraic subsets of parameter space. Projection and exceptional-parameter stratification can reveal where:

- solutions appear or disappear;
- root multiplicity changes;
- feasible sets change dimension;
- optima switch branches;
- topology changes.

This is useful in bifurcation-like analysis of algebraic models even when one does not need a full dynamical bifurcation theory.

## Computer algebra and theorem-assisted mathematics

Semialgebraic decision procedures are useful as exact subroutines inside larger symbolic systems:

- checking side conditions generated by simplification;
- proving equivalence of piecewise formulas;
- deciding signs of algebraic expressions under assumptions;
- validating symbolic integrals or extrema;
- reconstructing region relations;
- generating replayable certificates.

This is one reason `semialg` exposes both high-level APIs and lower-level CAD/algebraic objects.

## Statistics and data science

Although most statistical workflows are numerical, algebraic constraints arise in identifiable models, covariance/PSD conditions, polynomial likelihood equations, moment problems, and exact design questions. Semialgebraic techniques can characterize feasible parameter regions or prove that certain parameter combinations are impossible.

## Economics and game theory

Polynomial utility, equilibrium, feasibility, and comparative-statics models can produce quantified real inequalities. QE can, in principle, derive exact parameter conditions for existence or dominance. Practical tractability depends strongly on dimension and polynomial degree.

## Physics and engineering design

Geometric tolerances, linkage constraints, polynomial constitutive approximations, and exact inequality envelopes can all yield semialgebraic problems. Exact region projection is particularly valuable when eliminating internal design variables to obtain constraints on observable or controllable quantities.

## When semialgebraic methods are not the right tool

Generic CAD is usually not the best first choice for:

- hundreds or thousands of variables;
- problems whose essential structure is transcendental rather than algebraic;
- large convex programs already handled efficiently by numerical convex solvers;
- approximate problems where certified symbolic answers provide no benefit.

Semialgebraic computation is strongest when dimensions are moderate, exact boundaries matter, parameters are symbolic, or a proof-quality yes/no/geometric answer is required.
