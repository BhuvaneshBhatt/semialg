# Algebraic coefficients in critical loci

Exact algebraic coefficients occur when a geometric constraint uses a calibrated length such as `sqrt(2)`, an algebraic eigenvalue, or a selected real root of a polynomial. Treat these as elements of a fixed number field, with their embeddings preserved. They are different from free parameters: the coefficient `sqrt(2)` never vanishes, while a parameter `a` in `a*x` has a zero stratum that must remain in a real quantifier-elimination problem.

Critical-locus dimension, multiplier projection, polynomial validation and active-set reduction infer one common exact field for their coefficients. Rational input retains QQ arithmetic. Coefficients from several fields use a compositum, for example QQ(sqrt(2), sqrt(3)). Embedded `CRootOf` coefficients retain their selected root; bound symbols in their defining polynomials are shielded from coordinate generators. Symbolic parameters and transcendental coefficients are outside these fixed-number-field helpers, and no generic function-field substitution discards their exceptional strata.

The mathematical contract is illustrated in [the coefficient-field tests](https://github.com/BhuvaneshBhatt/semialg/blob/main/tests/test_remaining_capabilities.py): eliminating a multiplier from `multiplier = sqrt(2)*x` and `multiplier = sqrt(3)*y` gives the line `sqrt(2)*x - sqrt(3)*y = 0`. Its affine dimension is one; adding `x=1` gives dimension zero, and adding inconsistent point equations gives the empty-locus dimension -1. This is Krull dimension, not the vector-space dimension of a finite quotient algebra.

[SymPy's number-field interface](https://docs.sympy.org/latest/modules/polys/numberfields.html) supplies primitive elements, field membership and compatible embeddings. These operations complement algroots' finite quotient arithmetic. semialg does not provide integral bases, class groups, units or regulators. Such arithmetic is not needed to preserve exact coefficient fields in real geometry. Algebraic-domain inference can still be expensive for large composita, and this support does not promise fast optimization for every algebraic-coefficient input.

One-parameter branch continuation also keeps Sturm germs in the native K(t) coefficient representation. Taking the first nonzero native coefficient avoids artificial zero-over-zero expressions and unnecessary growth of algebraic units during repeated monic or square-free conversion. Algebraic endpoint, compositum-field and persistent-multiplicity cases have replay regressions.
